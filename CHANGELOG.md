# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented in this file.
The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/),
and the project uses [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

## [Unreleased]

### Changed

- `stats-ffi`: the core computes the findings with the thresholds the app sets (`NasCore::set_thresholds`, `demo_snapshots(now, thresholds)`, `standard_thresholds`) and recomputes them from the snapshot as read, so a changed threshold shows at once without a query, also for the snapshot of the test query of the add dialog, which the core takes over when the NAS is added. Snapshots name the volumes whose fill level counts (`capacity_volume_ids`). The apps take the findings of the core unchanged and no longer check fill level and disk temperature themselves (#85).

### Added

- Widgets: the widget „Alle NAS“ (large) shows a row per NAS, the worst first and at most three, with status dot, name, the bar of its fullest volume, the number of warnings (or „Stand 14:02“ of an old state, or the error) and the age of the last backup, then „+ N weitere NAS“, and below the 30 days of the fullest volume of all NAS as a chart; without history it says „Noch kein Verlauf“ until #67 delivers it. The widget „Füllstand“ (small) shows a bar per volume of the chosen NAS (the three fullest) with the sum of their sizes below, or „Stand 14:02“ in grey for an old state. It uses the NAS setting of the widget „NAS“; a volume choice is not offered. iPhone and Mac desktop; demo values are marked „DEMO“ (#38).
- Widgets: the widget „NAS“ in two sizes. Small shows status dot, name, the ring of the fullest volume and below it up to two lines: the first warning, then „voll in X Tagen“; once the state is old, „Stand 14:02“ in grey replaces the forecast below a warning and follows it without one. Medium shows status dot, name, model and the first warning on the left, the three fullest volumes as bars on the right and disk temperature, CPU, RAM and the last backup below. iPhone and Mac desktop; demo values are marked „DEMO“. The forecast shows once #67 delivers it (#24).
- `stats-ffi`: history, events, forecast, backup runs and the planner for Swift. Every query of `NasCore` runs through the planner of `stats-core`, also `refresh` (5 minutes, no longer waits for a running query) and the new manual `refreshNow` (30 s). `startPlanner` lets the core query on its own and calls a `StateListener` of the app after every finished query, also a failed one; `stopPlanner`, `setInterval` and `restorePoll` (time of the last query and block the app remembers) come with it. Every query goes into the history archive (in memory, or a file with `NasCore.withHistory(path:)`), with events by the thresholds set with `setThresholds` (#85) and „nicht erreichbar“ when the NAS gives no answer 15 minutes after the last snapshot (a refused login does not count); removing a NAS deletes its history. New calls `history` (metric and range), `events` (time range, one or all NAS), `backupRuns` and `forecast` (volume), records `Metric`, `HistoryRange`, `HistorySeries`, `HistoryPoint`, `NasEvent`, `EventKind`, `BackupRun`, `Forecast`. `NasCore.demo(now:)` brings the demo NAS with their history. `stats-core` reads events of a time range (`HistoryStore::events`) (#60).
- Widgets: the NAS widget has a setting „NAS“ (App Intent): „Automatisch“ (the default) shows the NAS with the worst state, a fixed NAS shows exactly that one, in demo mode marked „Demo“ in the list. A NAS that is not in the app any more shows the worst NAS and keeps the stored choice. The grey „Stand“ note follows the shown NAS. The volume choice for the fill level widget follows with #38 (#26).
- `stats-core`: forecast "full in X days" per volume (`forecast`, `HistoryStore::forecast`). A least-squares line runs through the daily values of the used space of the last 30 days; it needs at least 7 daily values (gaps do not count, the line uses the real distances) and a rising line, otherwise there is no trend (`None`), also when the volume would be full only after more than 10 years. The days are counted from the point of the line at today, not from the last value, so a large cleanup does not make the forecast jump; a line above the size of the volume gives 0 days. Result: days to full, date and growth in bytes per day. The demo data give DataVol1 "full in 143 days" (143.08 from the line); the apps round the days, the display follows with #67 (#9).
- `nas-model`: events from changes of state. `detect_events` compares a snapshot with the open events (not with the previous snapshot) and returns opened, raised and closed events for alerts, disks, volumes, fans, UPS and backups, with hysteresis (disk 3 °C, volume 1 point below the threshold; a fill event opens at 80 % and rises to critical at 90 %). `detect_unreachable` opens „nicht erreichbar“ after 15 minutes without a snapshot. `nas-demo` carries the events of the mockup (#10).
- `stats-core` crate: history archive in SQLite (`HistoryStore`). Each snapshot writes used and total space per volume, temperature per disk, CPU, memory, system temperature and throughput per interface, plus the last run of each backup task (a finished run does not go back to running). Every write rebuilds the touched UTC hours (min, mean, max) and days (a day older than the hourly values keeps its daily value) and prunes: raw samples after 7 days, hourly values after 90 days, daily values, backup runs and events never. `history` returns every sample for 24 hours, hourly values for 7 and 30 days and daily values for 1 year; events (#10) are stored with start and end (`write_events`) and the open ones per NAS are loaded for the detection after a restart (`open_events`); schema version 1 with stepwise migrations (#8).
- `stats-core` crate: polling planner that queries each NAS at most every 5 minutes (a longer interval may be set), keeps the last snapshot with error time and error on failures, retries after a network error only at the regular time, blocks a NAS after a rejected login, second factor or changed certificate until it is unblocked, and allows a manual refresh 30 s after the last query. It takes the last query time and the block the app remembers, hands every finished query to a `PollSink` and runs on tokio; tested with paused time and a fake provider. The apps are not connected yet (#60) (#11).
- `nas-demo` crate: deterministic demo data as in the mockup (Keller with QNAP TS-464, Büro with Synology DS923+, Homelab with TrueNAS CE 25.10), with 90 days of daily history from a fixed seed and daily backup runs. All times follow the given `now`; ids start with `demo-`, base URLs end in `.demo.invalid`. The DataVol1 history yields "full in 143 days" from a regression over the last 30 days (#22).
- `nas-http` crate: HTTPS layer for all providers with certificate pinning after the first contact (SHA-256 fingerprint), `CertificateChanged` with old and new fingerprint, 20 s time limit, no retries, no redirects, HTTPS only.
- `ProviderError::Unreachable` carries a detail for the log (#3).
- Project skeleton: Rust workspace with the `nas-model` crate, CI, setup script.
- `nas-model`: shared types for NAS, system status, volumes, disks, backups, UPS, fans, containers, alerts, interfaces, snapshots and samples, the `Provider` trait with `ProviderError`, and salted hashing of disk serials (#2).
- `provider-qnap`: login over `authLogin.cgi` with the session kept in memory, exactly one new login when the session is gone, `PermissionDenied` when the endpoint stays refused, a hard stop after a rejected login or required 2-step verification until the user logs in again or changes the credentials, and logout (#4).
- `provider-qnap`: system values from `sysinfo` (model, firmware with build, hostname, uptime, CPU, memory, system and CPU temperature, system and CPU fans), an alert when the system health is not `good`, and an offered firmware update, fetched at most once per hour. A part the account may not read stays empty. `SystemStatus` gains `memory_total_bytes` and `cpu_temperature_c` (#5).
- `provider-qnap`: throughput per network interface in bytes per second from `chart_func=bandwidth` (fallback `QSM40bandwidth`), interfaces without a link left out, and the containers of the Container Station with their own login and session cookie. A missing Container Station, an account without admin rights or a rejected login give an empty list without repeated logins. `nas-http` keeps cookies per NAS and can post JSON (#7).
- Xcode project via xcodegen (`apps/NasStats/project.yml`): iOS app `NasStats`, macOS menu bar app `NasStatsMac` without Dock icon, widget extensions per platform, App Group and keychain group. `stats-ffi` with the core version as first UniFFI call, `uniffi-bindgen`, `scripts/build-ffi.sh` for the XCFramework, `setup.sh` generates the project, CI job `Apps` builds both apps on the Xcode 27 runner (#13).
- `provider-qnap`: `QnapProvider` implements `Provider` and builds a snapshot from the system values; volumes, disks and the network follow with #52 (#12).
- `stats-ffi`: records for every type inside a snapshot (with `used_percent`, `worst_health` and a `demo` flag), `NasConfig`, `NasProblem` and `NasState`; `NasCore` to add, remove, query and re-credential NAS in memory, at most one query per NAS in 5 minutes, no query after a rejected login, second factor or changed certificate until new credentials, the last snapshot kept on errors; `fetch_fingerprint`, `demo_snapshot` and one tokio runtime for all network calls. Swift test `NasStatsMacTests` decodes every record and runs in the `Apps` CI job (#12).
- `provider-qnap`: volumes (size, used, RAID type, status code) from `chart_func=disk_usage` and disks (slot, model, hashed serial, temperature, SMART summary) from `qsmart.cgi`, with the status tables in code. Unknown codes give no health and a log line. Storage pools stay open: `disk_manage.cgi?store=…` answers a GET only with `unknown cmd format.` (#6).
- Apps: dialog „NAS hinzufügen“ on iPhone (sheet) and Mac (own window from the menu): QNAP address, user and password, the SHA-256 fingerprint of the certificate to confirm, one test query before saving and German texts for wrong password, 2-step verification, missing rights and an unreachable NAS. Each NAS is one keychain entry (iCloud Keychain only on request) and is registered with the core at every start; iPhone start screen and Mac menu list the stored NAS (#14).
- `provider-qnap`: `QnapProvider::snapshot` asks system values, storage, network and containers one after the other and fills volumes, disks, interfaces and containers. An HTTP error status of storage, network or containers leaves only that part empty; unreachable, timeout, rejected login and changed certificate fail the snapshot. Pools and backups stay empty (#52).
- `NasStore` in the apps: queries the NAS of `NasAccounts` (#14) every 5 minutes, keeps the block and the time of the last query across launches (no query at launch within 5 minutes, no retry of a refused login), counts the test query of the add dialog as the first query, and sorts them critical, warning, ok, unknown (an error lifts to warning, a block or a registration the core refused to critical). Add, remove and new credentials (user and password) go through the store; credentials the core refuses leave the old ones in the keychain. Demo mode shows the three demo NAS from the new FFI call `demo_snapshots` without queries, `onUpdate` is the hook for the widget snapshot (#23). Menu bar (with a demo mode toggle) and iPhone list show the state per NAS (#15).
- Mac menu bar: the NAS symbol takes the colour of the worst NAS (green, yellow, red; monochrome without NAS or with an unknown state) and, as chosen, shows the highest fill level of all volumes or the number of NAS with a warning. A left click lists the NAS, a right click offers Aktualisieren (only NAS last queried 5 minutes ago or longer), Anzeige, Bei Anmeldung starten (`SMAppService`), Einstellungen and Beenden. Store and symbol live in an app delegate, so the 5-minute queries run without an open menu or window; the provisional settings window holds display, login item, demo mode (moved there from the menu) and core version (#27).
- iPhone app without a Mac: due NAS are queried on return to the app and occasionally in the background (`BGAppRefreshTask`, no promised frequency), with a clearer text for „Lokales Netzwerk“. The last snapshot of every NAS is kept across launches (`saved_state`/`restore_state` in `stats-ffi`, `last-states.json` in the App Group), so away from home the app shows it with its time and „Nicht erreichbar“ instead of a red error; card, detail and widget use „Nicht erreichbar“ for a NAS that does not answer or times out, on the Mac too (#29).
- iPhone app: a tap on a card of the overview opens the detail screen of the NAS with model, firmware, uptime and an offered update, volumes (fill bar coloured by the thresholds of the settings, a warning in colour) and pools, disks (temperature in yellow from the threshold of the settings, SMART, power-on hours, standby), backups (QNAP without tasks: „Hybrid Backup Sync: Schnittstelle offen“), CPU, RAM and UPS, throughput per interface and containers (stopped first). Missing values show „n/a“, empty lists and a missing UPS leave out their section; the forecast per volume follows with #67 (#18).
- Apps: settings on iPhone (tab „Einstellungen“) and Mac (the settings window of the menu bar, ⌘,): rename a NAS, change its access, start a blocked NAS again once with the stored access („Erneut versuchen“), check a changed certificate and trust the new fingerprint after comparing old and new (#76), remove a NAS; query interval of 5, 10, 15 or 30 minutes per NAS, thresholds for fill level (warning, critical) and disk temperature that colour and sort the NAS, demo mode with a note that the values are made up, and a privacy note. Interval and thresholds survive a restart; invalid stored values fall back to the standard. „Aktualisieren“ in the menu bar follows the interval (#21).
- `nas-model`: findings per snapshot (`Snapshot::findings` with `Thresholds`): alerts, disks the NAS reports as not good or at 50 °C and above, volumes the NAS reports as not good or filled to 80 % (warning) and 90 % (critical) by default, fans, the UPS on battery (critical) and backups that failed or did not run for more than two days (snapshot tasks and the parity check never count as overdue), each with level, subject and German text, critical first. The fill counts only the first kind present of volume, pool, array and share (`capacity_volumes`). `stats-ffi` hands them to the apps in `Snapshot.findings` (#17).
- iPhone overview: one card per NAS, worst first, with status dot, name, model, vendor, the findings as lines in their colour, a fill bar per volume and the key figures; the head names the state of all NAS in one sentence and the age of the oldest state. Pull to refresh queries only NAS whose interval has passed (at least 5 minutes), a tap opens a placeholder of the detail (#18), and without NAS the empty state offers „NAS hinzufügen“. The health of a NAS (sort order, menu bar) now follows the findings; the core checks fill level and disk temperature against the thresholds of the settings (#21, #85), and the fill counts only the volumes the core counts, so a pool and its volume no longer count twice (#17).
- `qnap-capture`: records the answers of an own QNAP read-only (pinned certificate, one login, request list checked for commands) and anonymises them for the fixtures (#1).
- Widget snapshot in the App Group (`widget-snapshot.json`): after every new state and after a change of the query interval the app writes per NAS id, name, model, vendor, state, first finding, volumes with fill level, hottest disk, CPU, RAM, last backup and the time of the state, plus a demo flag and a schema version, and reloads the widgets only when the content changed. Forecast per volume and the 30-day fill history of the fullest volume are in the format but stay empty until #67. The placeholder widget reads it: worst NAS with status dot, „DEMO“, the first warning or the error, and „Stand 14:02“ in grey once the state is older than 30 minutes, or than the query interval plus 5 minutes if that is longer; demo data never turns grey (#23).

### Fixed

- The settings of each NAS show „Über iCloud-Schlüsselbund auf meine Geräte“ and switch it afterwards: the keychain entry moves to or from iCloud Keychain, the NAS stays reachable and the password is never shown or logged (#98).
- Fill bar: a total just below a unit boundary shows in the next unit („1,0 TB“ instead of „1.024,0 GB“); `capacity` and `bytes` share the rule for the unit (#86).
